Written 2026-09-22 against `5fbf9a165` from the Release Gate observation artifacts of `6f6006cfe`
(S1), `f33dd8118`, `f870feaf4` and `8fc704552` (F2c); `artifact:<name>/` refers to the
`alm-conformance-lane-<sha>` artifact of that head. Evidence only; the S2 design proposal beside this
file argues from it.

Evidence root, abbreviated `EV/` below: the four artifacts' unpacked directories, named
`alm-lane-6f6006cfe`, `f2c-lane-f33dd8118`, `f2c-lane-f870feaf4` and `f2c-lane-8fc704552`.

## 3 Loss stage per failure

Three distinct terminal symptoms, one chain:

**A. `receive-submission` times out (f870feaf4 rtc, 8fc704552 rtc, 8fc704552 fallback).**
Break point: **receiver inbound work drain → page dispatch.**

- Sender outbound admitted it: `commit-phases … commitOutcome=committed` (f870feaf4 rtc, t=16.57).
- Transport carried it: inbound `admission-outcome outcome=committed reason=admitted msgId=7e623339…`
  (t=25.41). Not a transport loss.
- Receiver inbound **never reserved the `dispatch-local` effect**: zero `claim-settled` with that
  `msgId`, and the only two `dispatch-local` claims in the window are `group-app-inbox:…`.
- Cause of the non-reservation: the receiver ran **two** inbound drains in 35.5 s (`dur` 14 934 and
  17 066). The admission landed 0.33 s before the second one finished, so it missed that batch's claim
  set, and the next batch never began inside the 9.7 s the harness had left.

**B. sender `observe-acknowledged-1` times out (f33dd8118 rtc + fallback, f870feaf4 fallback, 8fc704552 rtc/fallback).**
Break point: **receiver inbound drain, ahead of the ack send.** The 10 s budget must cover
sender-commit → transport → receiver admission → receiver batch start → the claims ahead of it →
`al.control.ack.v1` outbound commit → transport → sender inbound admission. In f33dd8118 rtc the ack's
own outbound commit alone cost 4 050 ms (t=32.14) and its claim 6 734 ms, and the page dispatch only
landed at 32.38 — 4.6 s _after_ the sender had already given up at 27.75.

**C. sender `observe-superseded-3` times out (6f6006cfe rtc, f870feaf4 ws).**
Break point: **sender outbound work drain, not the receiver.** `superseded` is decided inside the
outbound dispatch path (`packages/shared/alm/outbound/compute-al-outbound-dispatch.ts:203-205`), i.e.
only when the drain next _attempts_ send-3. In 6f6006cfe rtc the drain at t=38.01 reported
`claimed=2 completed=0 rescheduledCount=2` — the held sends were pushed to a retry time — and no drain
claimed send-3 again before the 3 s budget expired at 42.52. `observe-superseded-3` is the tightest
budget in the scenario and it is spent waiting for a rescheduled outbound attempt.

**Cascades (not independent failures).** In every cell whose sender died before
`supersede-release-*`, the receiver's `receive-replacement` timed out because the `drop`/`not-ready`
hold was still armed and the replacement never left the sender page. That is a consequence of A/B/C,
not a separate defect — worth stating because the cell files report both roles as failed.

## 4 Differences from the control

Comparing the green hosted cells (§2.5) against the failing ones. Nothing dismissed.

1. **Receiver `send-control` claim cost: 689–1 187 ms green vs 1 508–18 115 ms red.** A 15–26×
   spread. This is the single largest difference and it tracks the outcome perfectly (§2.6).
2. **Receiver inbound drain duration: 2.2–3.4 s green vs 5.9–22.8 s red.** The drain count in the
   window collapses from 5–7 to **2** in the three total-loss cells.
3. **`queueWait` on the submission's `dispatch-local` claim: 6 ms (6f6006cfe rtc) vs 2 670 ms
   (f33dd8118 rtc) vs no claim at all.** In the green cell the inbound queue was empty when the
   message landed.
4. **Sender→page latency: 2.3–6.4 s green vs 8.5 s–∞ red.**
5. **IndexedDB per-operation cost: 12.9–29.8 ms green vs 20.8–43.0 ms red.** Roughly 2×. The
   resulting drain-cost change is ~20×, so the amplification is strongly super-linear — the runner
   slowdown is the _input_, not the whole explanation.
6. **Retransmission pressure.** Green 6f6006cfe rtc still emitted one `origin=repair` retransmission
   of the submission at 22.41 and the receiver answered `not-handled/duplicate` at 23.18 — i.e. the
   ack round trip was already at the 10 s limit in the _passing_ cell (`durationMs=10004` vs a 10 000
   budget). Margin at the control point is zero, not comfortable.
7. **Ordering inside the inbound batch differs.** Green: the batch that carried the submission had
   `queueWait=6` and settled `dispatch-local` in 436 ms. Red: the same batch first ran
   `al.control.repair.v1` (6 829 ms) and `al.control.ack.v1` (6 734 ms) `send-control` claims, then
   the dispatch. `dispatch-local` claims themselves cost **0–501 ms in every cell** — the dispatch is
   never the slow thing.
8. **Receiver outbound `rtt` commit cost: 551–631 ms green vs 1 874–5 813 ms red**, 6–7 commits per
   window either way. These contend for the same per-session outbound commit lock
   (`rallar:al-outbound-commit:session-…`) that the `send-control` effects need.
9. **Sender outbound lock-hold median: 527–692 ms green vs 1 244–4 976 ms red.**
10. **RTC peer readiness: 4.0–13.3 s green vs 8.2–23.1 s red.** Consistent with runner load; it is
    _before_ the scenario and did not gate any loss (all connects returned `ok`).
11. **Hold/release wall-clock spacing.** Green ws: `supersede-hold` 23.33, send-3 27.65, send-4 32.06,
    `observe-superseded-3` resolves 35.37, `supersede-release` 35.86. Red rtc: same order, but the
    observe never resolves, so the release never runs. The _ordering_ is identical; only the timing
    differs.
12. **Verdict kinds are identical.** Across all 12 cells the receiver's admission verdict for every
    lifecycle message is `committed/admitted` (plus 2 `not-handled/duplicate`). No verdict kind is
    unique to the failing cells.
13. **No fault was armed when the first failures occurred.** f870feaf4 rtc's whole snapshot contains
    exactly one `fault.injected` event, belonging to `deadline-expiry`. The lifecycle scenario died at
    `observe-acknowledged-1`, before `cancel-hold`. The A-class loss is fault-free.

## 5 The six checks

**(a) Does the supersedence hold's release race the replacement's admission?** **No.** In every cell
that reached step 15, the order is `supersede-hold` → send-3 → send-4 → `observe-superseded-3` →
`supersede-release` (6f6006cfe rtc: 30.85 / 34.09 / 38.39 / 39.42 / never; 6f6006cfe ws: 23.33 / 27.65 /
32.06 / 35.37 / 35.86). The old send is still held when the replacement is admitted **by design**, and
`inject` with `remaining:0` disables the fault correctly
(`packages/shared/transport-faults/transport-fault-port.ts:84` + `:130` require
`remaining === 'until-cleared' || remaining > 0`). The failure is that the release step is never
_reached_, not that it races.

**(b) Is the submission/replacement admitted `pending` / `not-yet-in-sync` and then never delivered?**
**No — ruled out with evidence.** The string `not-yet-in-sync` does not occur in any of the 12
snapshots (`grep -c` = 0 in all). Across all cells the inbound admission verdicts are
`committed/admitted` 424, `pending/pending-admission` 84, `not-handled/control` 20,
`not-handled/duplicate` 2 — and **every one of the 84 `pending` verdicts is `overlay.topology`,
`client-state.*`, `group-state.event` or `rtc-signaling`**, never an `alm.conformance.*` typeId. The
S2 roadmap's "pending frame dropped on the receiver, sender retry does not fire" mechanism is not
what these artifacts show.

**(c) Is the message expired by TTL before the wait matches?** **No.** The lifecycle sends pass
`delivery: { ack: 'receiver' }` with no `ttlMs` (`create-alm-conformance-recipes.ts:450`, `:520`,
`:543`), so the browser default (30 s) applies, and the longest observed commit→wait-timeout span is
17.5 s (f870feaf4 rtc: 16.57 → 34.06). Across all 12 cells there are exactly **two**
`commitOutcome=expired` commits and both are `typeId=rtt`, not ALM traffic. What _does_ expire are
harness command budgets: `observe` (10 s / 3 s), `send` (10 s — 8fc704552 ws failed `send-2` at
10 352 ms), and the receiver `wait` (27 s).

**(d) Does an ACK-timeout / NACK retry loop consume the budget?** **Yes, as an amplifier.** The
sender retransmits (`origin=repair` commit of the submission's own `msgId`: 6f6006cfe rtc t=22.41,
fallback t=32.03 and t=48.41) and the receiver emits `al.control.nack.v1` / `al.control.repair.v1`
(f33dd8118 rtc t=17.59 `dur=6829`, t=24.90 `dur=3529`). Each such control message is a `send-control`
durable effect that performs a **full outbound IndexedDB commit inside the inbound drain**, so slow
delivery generates control traffic that makes delivery slower. But the loop is _triggered by_ the
latency, so it is positive feedback, not the origin.

**(e) Is the receiver's `wait` matching a selector the envelope does not satisfy?** **No.** In every
cell where a page message event exists, its payload is byte-identical to the wait's `equals`
(e.g. `{"marker":"delivery-lifecycle","specimen":"submission","carrier":"rtc"}` at 6f6006cfe rtc
t=15.40, and `{…"supersedence","carrier":"ws","revision":"replacement"}` at 6f6006cfe ws t=39.02), and
those waits passed. The failing waits have **no** matching page event at all. Timing cannot cause a
miss either: a positive wait scans the whole buffer on entry (`wait/wait-for-event.ts:58`) and again on
every change (`:108`), and `resolveLatestWaitEvent` walks the array from the end
(`wait/wait-event-match.ts:44-53`, loop at `:48`). No harness mismatch.

**(f) Does the RTC lane drop or reconnect mid-scenario?** **It drops, but after the loss.** f870feaf4
rtc: `lane-error`/`lane-close` at 28.36–28.74, i.e. 3 s _after_ the admission at 25.41 and coincident
with the sender recipe ending at 28.78 (page teardown). 8fc704552 rtc: 31.74–32.19, after the 27.32
admission. f33dd8118 rtc: `lane-close` at 28.70 — and the message still reached the page at 32.38,
served from the receiver's own store, which positively proves the loss is post-transport. 6f6006cfe
rtc: 44.15, long after delivery. In no cell does a lane event precede the message it would explain.

## 6 Hypothesis, alternatives, confirming experiment

### Primary hypothesis

**The hosted `delivery-lifecycle` failures are one defect: the receiver's inbound effect drain is a
single serial batch that interleaves `send-control` effects — each of which performs its own outbound
AL IndexedDB commit — with `dispatch-local` page deliveries, so the delivery latency a caller observes
is a whole drain cycle rather than the cost of the dispatch itself. On the hosted runner each
`send-control` claim costs 1.5–18 s instead of 0.7–1.2 s, which inflates the drain cycle from ~3 s to
7–23 s, and the recipe's `observe-acknowledged` (10 s), `observe-superseded` (3 s) and `receive-*`
(27 s) budgets fall on the wrong side of that cycle at different points depending on where the cycle
boundary lands — which is exactly why the failing step differs per run.**

Evidence **for**:

- `dispatch-local` claims cost 0–501 ms in every cell; `send-control` claims cost 689–18 115 ms. The
  work the caller is waiting for is never the slow work (§2.6, §4.7).
- The failing step is a monotone function of the `send-control` median across 12 cells on three heads
  (§2.6) — no cell breaks the ordering.
- f33dd8118 rtc shows the mechanism directly: batch starts 24.05, runs `al.control.repair.v1`
  (settles 24.90) and `al.control.ack.v1` (settles 32.26, its own commit 4 050 ms), then
  `dispatch-local` (settles 32.88). `queueWait=2670` but admission→dispatch 8.8 s: the missing 5.9 s
  is intra-batch serialization.
- The three total-loss cells ran only **two** drains in the window; the message landed after the
  second batch's claim set was fixed and the next batch never started.
- `INBOX_OUTBOX_ENGINE_MAX_IDLE_MS = 3_000` (`packages/shared/services/InboxOutboxEngine.ts:12`) and
  "The rotation runs a batch every engine round" (`inbound/al-inbound-message-runtime.ts:223`) mean the
  next round cannot start until the current batch ends, so the batch duration _is_ the cadence.
- The C-class failure is the same shape on the outbound side: `superseded` is only computed when the
  drain re-attempts the old send (`outbound/compute-al-outbound-dispatch.ts:203`), and the drain at
  6f6006cfe-rtc t=38.01 rescheduled both claims with `completed=0`.

Evidence **against / unexplained**:

- The regime label is not sufficient: f870feaf4 rtc lost the message at 20.8 ms/op (`normal`) while
  8fc704552 ws delivered at 41.4 ms/op (`slow`). The per-op median does not order the outcomes; the
  `send-control` claim cost does. So "the runner was slow" is an incomplete account, consistent with
  the brief's note that F2c's contention fix did not help.
- `pendingSharePercent` is 6–11 % in every F2c cell regardless of outcome, so the pending share is
  genuinely not the discriminator any more.
- Why one `send-control` commit costs 4–18 s when the same page's `commit-phases` reports
  `readOperationCount = 9–16` is not established here. The `browser-lock-hold` medians (1 244–4 976 ms
  on red senders) point at contention on `rallar:al-outbound-commit:session-…` with the page's own
  `rtt` probe commits (6–7 per window, 1.9–5.8 s each), but this pass did not prove causation.

### Alternatives, ranked

1. **RTT-probe commits are the real lock hog** (a variant, not a rival): the receiver's
   `typeId=rtt` outbound commits rose from 551 ms to 5 813 ms and are the only high-frequency non-ALM
   traffic on the contended lock. If so the fix is to keep RTT out of the durable AL commit path
   rather than to restructure the drain. Discriminator: whether a run with RTT probes suppressed keeps
   the `send-control` claim cost low.
2. **The next inbound round is not woken by an admission, only polled** — so the tail latency is a
   scheduling gap rather than batch serialization. `commitWork()` does call `restartScan()` +
   `this.work.committed()` (`inbound/al-inbound-message-runtime.ts:357-360`), but that is on the
   _replay_ path inside a batch; whether a transport-side admission commit announces to the inbound
   owner is not settled by these artifacts. Discriminator: the gap between the admission timestamp and
   the next batch's start when the engine is otherwise idle.
3. **Harness budgets are simply mis-set for a hosted runner.** `observe-superseded` at 3 s is
   asymmetric with `observe-acknowledged` at 10 s although both wait on a drain. Against: the maintainer
   ruling of 2026-09-11 (`docs/alm-observation-artifact.md`, "The budgets stay") forbids treating this
   as the fix, and three cells lose the message outright, which no budget can rescue.
4. **Control-message amplification is self-sustaining** — the repair/nack loop alone drives the drain
   past the budgets. Against: 6f6006cfe rtc also retransmitted and still delivered in 2.5 s.
5. **RTC lane instability.** Ruled out in §5(f).
6. **Pending / `not-yet-in-sync` retention.** Ruled out in §5(b).

### Smallest confirming experiment

**One variable, harness side, no product change:** re-run the hosted `rtc` cell with a new inbound
diagnostic that records, for each `dispatch-local` effect, the three timestamps the current contract
loses — the effect's `dueAtMs`, the batch's `startedAtMs`, and the claim's own `startedAtMs` — plus the
ordered list of `effectId`s the batch reserved. The existing `queueWaitMs` collapses the reservation
wait and the intra-batch wait into one number, which is precisely the discrimination needed: if
`claimStarted − batchStarted` dominates, the cause is intra-batch serialization behind `send-control`
(primary hypothesis); if `batchStarted − dueAt` dominates, the cause is round scheduling
(alternative 2). The three total-loss cells also need a "batch never started" witness, so the same
diagnostic should emit one event per inbound round that finds the row still unreserved.

This belongs to **the harness/diagnostic contract**, not the product: it adds an observation and
changes no behaviour, so it cannot itself alter the timing under test — except that the ALM
diagnostics relay is already known to be a per-op cost in the Playwright lane, so the new events must
be batched into the existing `effect-drain` payload rather than relayed per claim.

A second, independent one-variable probe for alternative 1: run the same cell with the RTC RTT probe
disabled and read whether the receiver's `send-control` claim median returns to the green band
(< 1 200 ms). That one is a **product-configuration** variable, so it is a diagnostic run, not a fix.

## 7 Open questions the S2 design must answer

**Identity.**

- An inbound `claim-settled` for `dispatch-local` carries `typeId: null` by contract
  (`packages/shared/alm/inbound/al-inbound-runtime-diagnostics.ts:54`), so a delivery can only be
  followed by `msgId`. Any S2 conformance assertion on dispatch must key on `msgId`, and the artifact
  analysis must stop filtering inbound claims by `typeId` — that filter silently hides every page
  dispatch.
- `send-control` effect ids are suffixed with the _control_ envelope's `msgId`
  (`inbound/prepare-al-inbound-commit-bundle.ts:74`), so an ack cannot be attributed to the message it
  acknowledges from the diagnostics alone. Which ack belongs to which delivery is currently
  unanswerable from an artifact; §6's experiment needs it.

**Receipts.**

- `observe → acknowledged` is a cross-page round trip with a 10 s budget, and the hosted control
  passed it at 10 004 ms. S2 must decide whether `acknowledged` is a state a conformance recipe may
  poll for at all on a hosted runner, or whether the receipt must be observed on the receiver (where
  it is local) and correlated afterwards.
- `superseded` is computed only on the next outbound dispatch attempt
  (`outbound/compute-al-outbound-dispatch.ts:203`). Is that intended, or should a replacement's
  admission settle the superseded predecessor synchronously in the same commit? The current shape
  makes a _local_ state observation depend on a drain cycle and a retry backoff, which is what breaks
  `observe-superseded-3`.
- What is the outbound retry delay for a dropped/held send? It comes from the QoS policy's
  `retryTracking.retryDelayMs` (`outbound/al-outbound-message-effects.ts:214`, `:333`) and the lane
  never states it, yet the `observe-superseded-3` budget is implicitly a bet on it.

**Retention of `not-yet-in-sync` frames.**

- The roadmap item is real but **invisible in this corpus**: zero occurrences in 12 hosted cells. S2
  needs a scenario that actually produces a receiver-side `not-yet-in-sync` admission, otherwise the
  fix will ship untested. The 84 `pending/pending-admission` verdicts observed are all infrastructure
  types and say nothing about ALM retention.

**WS ordering / carrier asymmetry.**

- The `ws` carrier asserts `transport-accepted` (sender-local) where `rtc` asserts `acknowledged`
  (cross-page) — `create-alm-conformance-recipes.ts:450-452`, `:578-580`. That single difference is why
  `ws` survives 1.5–1.9 s `send-control` costs that kill `rtc`. S2 should decide whether the two
  carriers are meant to prove the same property; as written the lane tests strictly less on `ws`.
- The receiver's four waits are strictly sequential and `absent-cancelled` blocks for 17 s
  (`:662`). That is harmless today only because a positive wait rescans the whole buffer. Any future
  change to `sinceEpochMs` semantics (`wait/wait-event-match.ts:67`) would turn this into a real
  ordering bug.

**Budgets.**

- The observe-budget classifier grants 10 s only to `expired`, `acknowledged` and `transport-accepted`
  (`:894`). Every other state — including `superseded`, which demonstrably depends on a drain — gets
  3 s. If the budgets stay fixed by maintainer ruling, S2 must either make those states settle
  synchronously or stop asserting them through a poll.
